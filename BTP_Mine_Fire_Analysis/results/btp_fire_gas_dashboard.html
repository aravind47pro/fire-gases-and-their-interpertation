🔬 Empirical Resolution of the 4 Core BTP Research Questions

Q1: Does CO increase when temperature increases?

Pearson r = +0.540 (Moderate) Non-Monotonic!

The relationship is non-monotonic. The highest temperature (80°C on 20-06-1987) produced low CO (0.022%) because residual O₂ was only 1.55%. Peak CO (0.813%) occurred at 64°C where O₂ was 4.22%. CO generation requires available oxygen.

Q2: Does O₂ decrease as temperature increases?

Pearson r = -0.370 (Weak-Negative) Leakage Governed

Oxidation consumes O₂ at elevated temperatures. However, residual O₂ behind seals is strongly influenced by surface barometric breathing and stopping leakage. For example, Sample 12 had 0.00% O₂ at 37°C.

Q3: Does H₂ increase with temperature?

Pearson r = +0.536 (Moderate) Thermal Threshold

Hydrogen indicates high-temperature coal heating (>250°C at the coal face) and the water-gas reaction. Peak H₂ (2.343%) occurred at 72°C. Samples below 55°C showed 0.000% detectable H₂.

Q4: Does Graham ratio increase with temperature?

Pearson r = +0.539 (Moderate) Oxidation Indicator

Graham ratio ($100 \cdot CO / \Delta O_2$) reflects oxidation intensity. While positively associated with temperature, it is non-monotonic due to oxygen starvation and cannot serve as an absolute direct thermometer.
